DriverInsights · Stage 1 metrics reference

Where every number on the dashboard actually comes from

This is a field-by-field map of the 24 metrics scoped into Stage 1: which CRM the data comes from, which table and field it reads, the math behind the headline number, and — for anything built on a guess, a placeholder, or a fixed default — the decision that still needs a client answer before it should be treated as final.

Three systems feed this platform today: HubSpot (marketing/lead funnel), Salesforce (sales pipeline), and QuickBooks (billing and financials). Each org connects its own copies; data lands in that org's own database, gets lightly reshaped, and every metric below is computed fresh from that stored copy — never a live call to the CRM at request time.

Value Creation

VC–01 … VC–14

Revenue quality and growth. Everything here traces back to two QuickBooks building blocks: the Invoice-based customer revenue roll-forward (who paid what, month to month) and the PLSnapshot table (a stored copy of the QuickBooks Profit & Loss report, refreshed once per sync rather than fetched live).

VC-01

Gross Revenue Retention

Done

Of the recurring money you had at the start of the month, how much did you keep — counting only cancellations and downgrades, ignoring upsells.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksInvoice.customerRecordId, amountPer-customer monthly revenue, this month vs. last
GRR% = (Starting revenue − Cancellations − Downgrades) ÷ Starting revenue × 100
Open items
  • No "why" behind churn. Salesforce could tag a deal Type = 'Churn' to explain a drop — not pulled today.
  • 90% alert line is fixed in code, same for every client. Not adjustable per org.
VC-02

Net Revenue Retention

Done

Same idea as GRR, but upsells count in your favor too — so this number can go above 100%.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksInvoice.customerRecordId, amountSame roll-forward as VC-01, plus the "expansion" leg
NRR% = (Starting − Cancellations − Downgrades + Expansion) ÷ Starting × 100
Open items
  • 90% / 100% alert lines are fixed in code, not adjustable per org.
VC-03

New-Logo Revenue Growth

Done

Growth of revenue from brand-new customers only, quarter over quarter.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksInvoice — customers with $0 prior historyIdentifies genuinely new customers (win-backs excluded)
Growth% = (This quarter's new-logo revenue − prior quarter) ÷ prior quarter × 100
Open items
  • No Salesforce cross-check. A deal tagged Type = 'New Business' would confirm the customer is really new — currently trusts QuickBooks history alone.
  • No "vs. plan" comparison — no revenue-target data exists anywhere in the system (see Decisions, below).
VC-04

COGS % and Mix

Done

Cost of actually delivering the product, as a share of revenue — split by product line, and by whether costs rose (rate) or sales shifted to a cheaper-margin product (mix).

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.income, .cogs (per Class)Revenue and cost of goods sold, by product line
COGS% = COGS ÷ Revenue × 100 Mix% = product revenue ÷ total revenue × 100
Open items
  • Cost categories are guessed from account names (keyword match, e.g. "rent" → G&A) — no per-org review step exists yet.
  • No peer-company comparison — needs a market-data feed, entirely outside the 3-CRM scope.
VC-06

CAC & Payback Period

Done

What it costs to win one new customer, and how many months it takes to earn that cost back.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.expenses (tagged "sales & marketing")Total acquisition spend
QuickBooksInvoice roll-forward — new-logo count & revenueCustomers won, and their typical revenue
CAC = S&M spend ÷ new customers won Payback (months) = CAC ÷ (avg. new-customer monthly revenue × gross margin)
Open items
  • No per-channel breakdown (Google / Meta / LinkedIn) — needs ad-platform connectors, out of scope. Blended figure only.
  • Salesforce already stores LeadSource/deal type but this metric doesn't use them yet.
VC-07

LTV : CAC — New Customers

Done

Is a new customer worth more than it cost to acquire them? Pure arithmetic on top of three other metrics — no new data pull of its own.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
DerivedVC-01 (churn) + VC-04 (margin) + VC-06 (CAC)All three feed straight into the formula below
LTV = monthly revenue × gross margin ÷ monthly churn rate (capped at 7 years) Ratio = LTV ÷ CAC
Open items
  • None of its own — inherits whatever gaps sit in VC-01 / VC-04 / VC-06.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.expenses (tagged "G&A")Overhead cost total

VC-10

EBITDA Margin Trend

Done

Core profitability, the number a PE firm looks at first — and whether it's trending up or down.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.income/.cogs/.expenses + sectionTotals.NetOperatingIncomeOperating income, with depreciation/amortization added back
EBITDA = Operating income + D&A Margin% = EBITDA ÷ Revenue × 100
Open items
  • Only the "shrunk two quarters running" alert works — plan- and peer-comparison legs have nothing to compare against yet.
VC-12

Recurring Revenue % of Total

Done

Share of revenue that's contractually recurring vs. one-off — a real driver of company valuation.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.income (accounts matched by keyword: "subscription", "recurring"…)Splits income into recurring vs. non-recurring
Recurring% = Recurring revenue ÷ Total revenue × 100
Open items
  • Same keyword-guess risk as VC-04/06/09 — an account like "Platform Fees" would be missed and miscounted as non-recurring.
VC-13

Revenue Growth (Recurring vs. Non-Recurring)

Done

Splits total revenue growth into its recurring and one-off pieces, to catch growth that's being propped up by lumpy one-time deals.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.income — current, prior, and year-ago quarterSame recurring/non-recurring split as VC-12
Growth% = (This period − prior period) ÷ prior period × 100 — for total, recurring, and non-recurring separately
Open items
  • Fixed 2026-09-12. Was computable:false on the seeded org — the year-over-year comparison needs a real quarter of data from 12 months back, and the dataset only had ~13 months of history. Extended to 26 months.
  • Inherits VC-12's classification risk; plan-comparison alert legs have nothing to compare against.
VC-14

LTV : CAC — Whole Customer Base

Done

Same LTV:CAC test as VC-07, but across every active customer rather than just new ones — the trailing-12-month view.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot + Invoice roll-forward, 4 trailing quartersTrailing-12-month CAC and average revenue per customer
LTV = avg. monthly revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ monthly churn Ratio = LTV ÷ CAC (trailing 12 months)
Open items
  • The "falling two quarters running" alert is approximated as a simpler one-point check — not a true two-quarter trend yet.

Capital & Balance Sheet

CB–05, 07, 10

Cash health. Sourced from three more QuickBooks reports — Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, and the A/R & A/P Aging reports — stored the same way as the P&L data above.

CB-05

Cash Position & Runway

Done

Cash on hand today, and how many months it lasts at the current burn rate — the only metric in the whole system checked daily rather than monthly.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksCashBalanceSnapshot.unrestrictedCash, asOfDateBank-account balance, one row per sync
Monthly burn = (oldest cash − latest cash) ÷ days between × 30 Runway (months) = latest cash ÷ monthly burn
Open items
  • No daily scheduler exists — this table only refreshes when someone reconnects or manually resyncs, not automatically every day as the "daily" cadence implies.
CB-07

Free Cash Flow Conversion

Done

How much of EBITDA actually turns into real cash in the bank.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.operatingCashFlow, .capExPrimary source, from the Cash Flow report
QuickBooks (fallback)accountsReceivable/Payable, inventoryValue, netFixedAssets deltasUsed only when a company's books can't produce a real Cash Flow statement
FCF = Operating cash flow − CapEx Conversion% = FCF ÷ EBITDA × 100
Open items
  • One alert leg can never fire — it needs a leverage/debt metric (CB-01) that's a later-stage build, not in Stage 1 at all.
  • Report-parsing assumptions haven't been checked against a real, non-sandbox QuickBooks company yet.
CB-10

Cash Conversion Cycle

Done

How many days cash is tied up before it comes back — time to collect from customers, plus time stock sits, minus time you take to pay suppliers.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.accountsReceivable, .accountsPayable, .inventoryValueThe three legs of the cycle
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.income, .cogsDenominators (revenue, cost of goods)
Days to collect = A/R ÷ Revenue × days Days in inventory = Inventory ÷ COGS × days Days to pay = A/P ÷ COGS × days Cycle = collect + inventory − pay
Open items
  • Companies with no inventory correctly show "0, hidden" rather than an error — this is by design, not a gap.
  • Report-parsing assumptions unverified against a live (non-sandbox) QuickBooks company.

Customer & Market

CM–02 … CM–08

Pipeline and funnel health. Sourced from HubSpot's contact/lifecycle data, Salesforce's opportunity pipeline, and — for concentration risk — QuickBooks customer relationships.

CM-02

Customer Concentration

Done

Share of trailing-12-month revenue coming from your 10 biggest customers — grouping subsidiaries under their parent company so a group isn't undercounted as several small accounts.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
QuickBooksQuickBooksCustomer.parentRecordIdRolls up subsidiaries to their parent before ranking
QuickBooksInvoice — trailing 12 months, by customerRevenue per customer group
Top-10% = revenue of 10 largest customer groups ÷ total revenue × 100
Open items
  • Salesforce's own Account.ParentId is synced but unused here — QuickBooks' own hierarchy turned out to be enough on its own.
CM-03

Competitive Win Rate

Done

Win rate specifically in deals where a named competitor was in the running, tracked per competitor.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
SalesforceDeal.competitors (custom field, or the standard OpportunityCompetitor object — org-configurable)Which competitor(s) were named on a deal
Win rate% = Wins ÷ (Wins + Losses) × 100, per competitor, trailing 2 quarters
Open items
  • Fixed 2026-09-12. Was computable:false on the seeded org — the competitor data existed, but the org's salesforceCompetitorSource setting had simply never been configured. Set to 'junction' mode.
  • Otherwise none outstanding — both ways an org might track competitors in Salesforce are now supported, configured per org.
CM-04

Pipeline Coverage Ratio

Partial

Qualified pipeline closing next quarter, measured against next quarter's sales target.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
Salesforce / HubSpotDeal.amount, .stage, .closeDateOpen pipeline closing next quarter (numerator — this part works)
Salesforce / HubSpotPipelineStageDefinition — earliest open stage = unqualifiedGuesses which stage counts as "qualified"
Missing entirelyNext quarter's sales targetThe denominator — doesn't exist anywhere in the system
Coverage = qualified pipeline closing next quarter ÷ next quarter's target
Open items
  • Structurally incomplete. Only the numerator can be shown — the ratio itself cannot exist until sales-target data is captured somewhere.
CM-05

Marketing-Sourced Pipeline & Revenue %

Done

Share of qualified pipeline and closed revenue that started from marketing, vs. sales outbound, partners, or referrals.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
HubSpotContact.analyticsSource (hs_analytics_source)Original lead channel
Marketing-sourced% = marketing-originated qualified pipeline ÷ total qualified pipeline × 100
Confirmed permanently not-computable on any seeded/demo org (2026-09-12). This needs deals actually created in a real past calendar month. HubSpot's createdate is read-only on create ("READ_ONLY_VALUE") — every seeded deal gets stamped "now," which this metric's own trailing-window logic always excludes. Not a code bug — a real, actively-used client's HubSpot naturally has this history already and would compute fine.
Open items
  • HubSpot-only. No equivalent field is pulled from Salesforce, so this metric under-represents orgs whose lead-source data lives there instead.
CM-06

Funnel Conversion Rates

Done

Lead → MQL → SQL → Won — what share of each cohort actually advances to the next stage.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
HubSpotContact.lifecycleStageHistoryEvery stage transition, by date
SalesforceDeal stage historyThe SQL → Won leg
Stage conversion% = records advancing to next stage ÷ records entering that stage × 100, grouped by entry-month cohort
Confirmed permanently not-computable on any seeded/demo org, both providers (2026-09-12). Needs contacts that entered MQL/SQL in a real past month. Same wall as CM-05 — a property's change-history timestamp is stamped at the real API-call time and cannot be backdated through the standard API. A real client's genuine usage history over time is unaffected.
Open items
  • Which lifecycle value counts as "MQL" vs. "SQL" is fixed in code, not something each client can configure for their own naming.
CM-07

Done

Pipeline and gross profit generated per marketing dollar spent — meant to be broken out by channel, currently blended only.

SourceTable / fieldUsed for
HubSpotContact.analyticsSourceAttribution (same field as CM-05)
QuickBooksPLSnapshot.expenses (marketing category)Total marketing spend
Pipeline ROI = marketing-sourced pipeline ÷ marketing spend Profit ROI = marketing-sourced revenue × gross margin ÷ marketing spend

  • Blended only — a true per-channel number needs Google/Meta/LinkedIn ad-spend connectors, confirmed out of scope.
  • HubSpot's own Campaigns API (recorded campaign cost) isn't pulled either — the connected OAuth scopes don't currently include it.

Decisions needed from the client

grouped by theme, not by metric

Most gaps above repeat across many metrics — so instead of asking the same question 20 times, here's every open decision grouped once, with the metrics it affects tagged underneath.

Scope conflict

Are CB-15, CM-01, and ER-05 actually required to call Stage 1 complete?

The original allocation lists all three as Stage-1 deliverables, but the build treated them as fully out of scope — no data model, no calculation, nothing. Either the original scope was aspirational and should be revised, or these three still need a connector/upload path scoped and built (NPS survey tool for CM-01; a bank-feed or cap-table source for CB-15; a defined data source for ER-05) before Stage 1 can be signed off as done.

CB-15CM-01ER-05

Configuration

Should alert thresholds (90% retention, 18-month payback, 3.0x LTV:CAC, 40% concentration, etc.) be adjustable per client, or is one fixed default acceptable?

Every threshold today is hardcoded in the backend, identical for every org. The original spec calls these "firm-adjustable defaults" — building a real per-org settings store is a separate, not-yet-scoped piece of work.

Missing feature

VC-04VC-06VC-09VC-10VC-13CM-04CM-05CM-08

Data quality risk

Should we build a per-org, editable mapping for which QuickBooks accounts count as Sales & Marketing / G&A / Recurring — before onboarding a real (non-test) client?

Right now this is guessed from account-name keywords. Already found real gaps in testing: an account named "Facebook Ads" wouldn't match the marketing keyword list; "Monthly Plan Revenue" wouldn't match the recurring-revenue list. On a real client's chart of accounts, this risk is much higher than on our test data — and any editable mapping needs version history so an edit never silently rewrites a past quarter's reported numbers.

VC-04VC-06VC-09VC-10VC-12VC-13CM-07

Connector scope

Do we add ad-platform connectors (Google/Meta/LinkedIn) and a HubSpot Campaigns integration, or accept "blended only" as the final answer for per-channel marketing metrics?

Per-channel CAC and marketing ROI both need real ad spend by channel — a connector family entirely outside HubSpot/Salesforce/QuickBooks. Confirmed the currently-connected HubSpot OAuth scopes don't even include Campaigns access. This is a real scoping decision, not a small fix.

VC-06CM-07

Cross-CRM polish (low urgency)

Worth wiring in Salesforce fields that are already synced but currently unused, to sharpen a few metrics?

Salesforce's Opportunity.Type, LeadSource, and Account.ParentId are already pulled into the database for other reasons but sit unused by these specific metrics. Using them would add a confirming cross-check, not fix a broken number — nice-to-have, not blocking.

VC-01VC-03VC-06CM-02

Platform limitation

CM-05/CM-06 can never show real numbers on demo/synthetic data — worth testing HubSpot's bulk Import API as a workaround, or accept this as a permanent demo-data limitation?

Confirmed live (2026-09-12): HubSpot rejects any attempt to backdate createdate on record creation ("read only property; its value cannot be set"). This blocks CM-05 entirely and CM-06 on both providers, on any one-shot seeded dataset — a real, actively-connected client is not affected, since genuine usage naturally accumulates this history over real calendar time. HubSpot's separate Import API is built for migrating historical CRM data and might support backdated property history, but this wasn't tested — unconfirmed whether it fakes a sequence of historical stage changes or just a backdated current state.
